# A Story of Asserting My Rights: My Experience in a USTC Study Room

## English Speech Script

---

Good morning/afternoon, everyone.

Today, I want to share a personal story with you. It's a story about a small battle I fought — not against a huge enemy, but for something simple and fundamental: my right to a quiet place to study. The title of my presentation is **"A Story of Asserting My Rights: My Experience in a USTC Study Room."**

---

**Let me set the scene for you.**

Imagine this. It's a typical evening during exam week here at USTC. The air is thick with the stress of final exams. I'm in one of our university's study rooms — a place that's supposed to be a sanctuary for students like me who need to focus. The lights are bright, the room is mostly quiet, filled with the soft rustling of papers and the gentle hum of air conditioning. My goal was simple: to review my notes and prepare for an important exam the next day.

But then... disruption. A student nearby started playing a mobile game — loudly. The sound effects, the voice chat, the tapping on the screen — it all cut through the silence like a knife. I couldn't concentrate. My notes blurred before my eyes. Minutes turned into what felt like hours, and my frustration grew.

---

**So, what did I do?**

After what felt like an eternity, I decided I had to do something. I took a deep breath, gathered my courage, and walked over to him. Now, I knew that being confrontational would only make things worse. So, I tried to approach him with a friendly and calm demeanor. I leaned over slightly and said:

*"Excuse me, I'm really sorry to bother you, but would you mind keeping the noise down a bit? I'm trying to study for my exams, and it's a little hard to concentrate."*

The key here was that I made it a personal request, not a complaint or an accusation.

---

**And what happened next?**

To my relief, his reaction was not what I feared. He looked up, surprised, and his face immediately turned red with embarrassment. He said, *"Oh my god, I'm so sorry! I didn't realize how loud I was being. I'll stop right away."* And he did. He immediately turned off the game, put his phone on silent, and slipped it into his bag. The noise stopped. The peace returned. I thanked him, went back to my seat, and was finally able to focus on my work. I felt a huge sense of relief, but also a feeling of empowerment. I had solved the problem with just a few polite words.

---

**But this experience made me think... What if I had stayed silent?**

First, I would have lost valuable study time — which is so precious during exam week. Second, my stress levels would have skyrocketed, making it even harder to learn. Third, that student would have continued to disturb not just me, but everyone else around him. And finally, I would have sent a message to myself that my needs and my right to a good study environment didn't matter. Staying silent would have been a lose-lose situation for everyone.

---

**So, how can we prevent this kind of situation from happening?**

I have three suggestions.

**First, start with yourself.** We can all contribute to a better environment by being more mindful of our own behavior. If you want to watch a video or listen to music, use headphones. If you need to have a conversation, take it outside. If you know you need absolute silence, choose a designated quiet zone. Small changes create a respectful and productive shared space. By being a good example, we can inspire others to do the same.

**Second, communicate with kindness.** When you encounter a problem like this, assume the other person has good intentions. They might genuinely not realize they are being disruptive. Instead of saying, *"You're being too loud!"*, try using "I" statements like, *"I'm finding it difficult to concentrate."* Put yourself in their shoes. Being polite and respectful is almost always more effective than being angry or aggressive. Remember, you have every right to speak up for a peaceful study environment — but always do so with kindness.

**Third, know when to seek help.** If a polite request doesn't work — if the person refuses to communicate or becomes hostile — don't hesitate to involve a librarian, a security guard, or a staff member. They are there to help maintain order. You can also provide feedback to the university, perhaps suggesting better signage or creating more designated silent zones as a long-term solution.

---

**So, what's the takeaway from my story?**

It's that asserting your rights isn't about being selfish or causing trouble. It's about protecting the shared environment that we all rely on. My small act of courage made a big difference — for me and for everyone else in that room. It taught me that my voice matters.

And I hope it teaches you the same.

**Your voice matters. Use it — and use it constructively — to make your environment a better place for everyone.**

Thank you.

---

*Estimated delivery time: 5–7 minutes*
